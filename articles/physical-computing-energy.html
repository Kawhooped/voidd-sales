The Physical Computing & Energy Idea

26 Sep 2026

Core thought

We tend to assume that energy, batteries, computers, and intelligence must all be built around electricity.

But perhaps electricity is only one implementation.

The deeper principle is that physical systems naturally contain differences and movements that want to resolve themselves:

  • pressure wants to equalize
  • heat flows from hot to cold
  • materials expand and contract
  • compressed objects seek equilibrium
  • concentrations diffuse
  • objects move and vibrate
  • day becomes night and temperatures cycle
  • human bodies constantly produce motion and heat

Instead of treating these processes as waste, we could engineer systems that harvest tiny amounts of their energy continuously.

A different kind of battery

Imagine a device that a person squeezes once.

The squeeze compresses a material, spring, gas, fluid, or other energy-storage mechanism. Instead of releasing that energy quickly, a carefully engineered mechanism lets it expand extremely slowly.

The stored energy could then drive a tiny generator for hours or days.

The same principle could be combined with other energy sources:

human movement + vibration + sunlight + temperature changes + pressure + chemical storage

Rather than asking for one magical infinite battery, the device could continually collect many tiny energy contributions.

The battery becomes less of a finite fuel tank and more of an energy buffer.

The desert example

A desert naturally produces a large temperature cycle:

day → heating → expansion
night → cooling → contraction

A properly engineered material or mechanism could potentially turn those repeated changes into mechanical motion and ultimately electricity.

The important idea isn't simply "use heat."

It is:

«Find a naturally recurring physical imbalance and put a machine between the two states.»

Distributed energy harvesting

Instead of putting one large energy harvester into a phone, imagine thousands of tiny ones distributed throughout it.

A module could capture:

  • movement
  • vibration
  • pressure
  • thermal changes
  • light
  • other environmental energy

Each contribution might be tiny.

But the phone could accumulate them in capacitors or chemical storage and use them when needed.

The goal isn't necessarily to produce a large amount of energy at once.

It is to never stop collecting energy.

Computation Without Electricity

There is another step to the idea.

Computers ultimately manipulate states.

We call those states 0 and 1.

But 0 and 1 don't inherently require electrons.

They only require two reliably distinguishable physical states.

For example:

  • position A = 0    position B = 1
  • pressure A = 0    pressure B = 1
  • light off = 0    light on = 1
  • magnetic orientation A = 0    magnetic orientation B = 1
  • chemical state A = 0    chemical state B = 1

Therefore, the deeper abstraction isn't electricity.

It is:

state → transformation → state

A sufficiently controllable physical system could theoretically perform logic through movement itself.

A tiny mechanical element could move into one position or another.
Its position could cause another element to move.
That element could trigger another.

Eventually, physical interactions could implement logic, memory, routing, and computation.

The computer would still be a computer.
The substrate would simply be different.

The Larger Possibility

This suggests a different way of thinking about future technology.

Instead of designing everything around electricity first, ask:

«What physical processes are already happening, and how can we make them perform useful work or computation?»

Energy and information might even become intertwined.

A physical movement could simultaneously:

  • store energy
  • represent information
  • trigger a logical operation
  • transmit a signal

The hierarchy could become:

Physics → physical states → computation → algorithms → AI

AI would not necessarily disappear if the physical substrate changed.
It could become more deeply integrated with the physical world.

The AI remains the intelligence.
The physical substrate becomes the machinery through which that intelligence exists and acts.

The fundamental invention question

Rather than asking:

«How do we make a better battery?»

or:

«How do we make a better computer?»

ask:

«What naturally wants to move, change, equalize, expand, contract, or transform—and how can we place a machine in that process?»

That question could lead to entirely different kinds of batteries, computers, sensors, and machines.

The ideas here are a research direction, not established technology. The engineering challenge is determining which physical processes contain enough usable energy, can be controlled efficiently, can survive enormous numbers of cycles, and can actually be manufactured economically.

But the conceptual starting point is simple:

«Don't fight the physics. Find the movement that is already happening, and learn how to use it.»
